This portion of the website is dedicated to data visualizations. I share acoustic surfaces and power spectra generated from experimental measurements of supersonic jets.

Abbreviation key

  • M - Mach number
  • AR - Aspect ratio (ratio between longest and shortest dimension)
  • TR - Temperature ratio (ratio between stagnation temperature at nozzle exit vs ambient temperature of room/atmosphere)
  • NPR - Nozzle Pressure Ratio (Ratio between stagnation pressure vs ambient pressure at nozzle exit
  • Rect - Supersonic rectangular nozzle
  • Diam - Supersonic diamond nozzle

Quick Guide to Supersonic Jet Acoustics

Key Parameters:

  • NPR (Nozzle Pressure Ratio): The ratio of stagnation pressure inside the nozzle to the ambient pressure outside. Higher NPR generally means higher downstream noise levels and different sound characteristics.
  • OASPL (Overall Sound Pressure Level): A measure of the total acoustic noise levels at a specific location in space.
  • Coordinates: Data is displayed relative to the jet exit, in nozzle equivalent diameters (D_eq)—a reference unit of length, like an inch.

Visualization Modes:

  • Measurement Points: Displays the discrete microphone positions where data was collected.
  • Filled Surface: Provides a continuous surface by connecting neighboring measurements. Note: This interpolation highlights spatial trends and does not increase the actual angular resolution of the original measurements.

How to interact: Use your mouse to rotate the 3D surface, scroll to zoom, and hover over the surface to see specific decibel values. Note: Colors are normalized per specific NPR case to highlight spatial trends; comparisons should be made between nozzles only at the same NPR.

Azimuth-resolved spectra

Because each azimuthal reconstruction is performed at a fixed NPR (fixed Uj and Deq), we plot three-dimensional spectra in frequency (Hz). In this setting f and St = fDeq/Uj are related by a constant factor, so using f preserves all physics while keeping the 3-D visualizations and tone/harmonic identification straightforward.

Isentropic Jet Velocity:
The jet exit velocity Uj is calculated based on the nominal NPR for a cold expanded jet:
Uj = √[ (2 γ R T0 / (γ - 1)) × (1 - NPR-(γ - 1) / γ) ]
Constants: γ = 1.4, R = 287 J/(kg K), T0 = 295 K.
